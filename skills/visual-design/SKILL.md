# Skill: Visual Design & UI

Use for art direction, layout, hierarchy, responsive UI, icons, typography, asset prompts and final visual review.

## Visual thesis
Premium cartoon junk-surrealism: readable toybox objects, absurd combinations, tactile backpack materials, strong silhouettes, saturated accents over controlled dark/neutral structure. Weird, not visually random.

## Hierarchy
At any gameplay moment the eye order should be:
1. current danger/boss state;
2. backpack and active interactions;
3. critical resources/actions;
4. secondary discovery/meta information.

## Rules
- Never rely on color alone for rarity, validity or status.
- Use consistent tag iconography and glow language.
- One item = one dominant silhouette/joke. Avoid overdesigned icons.
- Keep detailed art inside clear card/grid framing so chaos remains legible.
- UI panels need consistent corner radii, stroke weight, spacing and shadow language.
- Final UI must not look like default engine widgets.
- Reserve strongest neon/high-saturation effects for active synergies, rarity reveals and boss interference.

## Responsive targets
Design landscape-first but support narrow mobile web. Critical controls must remain visible without browser-edge collisions or unreadably small text. Respect safe areas.

## Asset acceptance
An asset is not final until it reads at gameplay size, has a clean silhouette, matches the palette/material language, and survives both bright VFX and dim backgrounds.

## Originality
Do not reproduce recognizable copyrighted meme characters, logos, branded products or another game's signature UI. Capture absurd internet energy through original designs.
